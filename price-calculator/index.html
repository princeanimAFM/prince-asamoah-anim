Questions

Why pay import VAT? When I buy from Shein the VAT is already paid.

Right. VAT is only paid once, but someone has to collect it. When you buy from Shein UK, Shein adds the 20% VAT to its price and pays it to HMRC for you. That's why you don't pay anything extra.

A designer in Ghana doesn't charge UK VAT, so their price has none in it. The UK collects that same 20% when the goods come in. That is import VAT. It isn't a second VAT. It's the one VAT, collected at the border instead of at the till.

When he then sells the item to a UK customer, there are two cases:

  • Not VAT registered (the normal start): he doesn't add VAT to his price. The import VAT he paid is simply one of his costs, and the calculator counts it.
  • VAT registered: he adds 20% VAT on his selling price and claims back the import VAT he paid. In the end, VAT is only charged once on the full selling price.
The courier only goes Ghana to UK. Why does origin matter?

Customs duty depends on where the item was made, not where it was sent from. Items made in Ghana are duty-free under the UK–Ghana trade agreement, but only if the paperwork says so. Without it, UK customs charges the normal rate (about 12% on clothing).

The fix is one sentence on the designer's invoice, the “statement on origin”. GOV.UK has the exact wording for the UK–Ghana agreement. If the courier clears customs and quotes an all-in price, ask whether they already handle this.

Why send items from Ghana in batches?

Shipments worth £135 or less follow different VAT rules: the overseas seller is meant to charge UK VAT at the point of sale, which a Ghanaian designer can't easily do. Sending one combined shipment a week worth more than £135 keeps it simple, with import VAT paid once at the border. Most couriers also price better per kg on larger loads.

How is the customs value worked out?

UK customs charge duty and import VAT on the item's price plus the cost of getting it to the UK. The calculator works it out like this:

  • Customs value = designer's price + courier cost
  • Duty = customs value × duty rate
  • Import VAT = 20% × (customs value + duty)

The transfer fee, packaging and UK posting are his costs, but customs don't charge on them.
