# Is It a Sin to Be Angry, or to Experience Emotions?

*"Be ye angry, and sin not." (Ephesians 4:26)*

---

## The question

Some teenagers asked me a question. We see a lot of evil done from anger, hatred, jealousy and so on.
Is it a sin to be angry, or to experience emotions? If not, what is the purpose of emotions?

## The answer: emotions are not sin

Feeling our emotions is not the sin. What we do with them can be. And they have a purpose.

Even Jesus was angry. He overturned the tables in the temple. He called the Pharisees hypocrites,
liars and whited sepulchres. "He looked round about on them with anger, being grieved for the
hardness of their hearts" (Mark 3:5; see also Matthew 23:27 and John 8:55). If the sinless Christ felt
anger, anger itself cannot be sin.

## Knowledge and understanding

People who are powerful know something that gives them that power, and people who are foolish lack
something that could make them wise. Your mind is passive in situations where you have inadequate
understanding, and active when you have adequate knowledge.

Your emotions have a reason to them. That is why God also gave us a mind to reason and understand.

## What our emotions are for

**Anger and compassion.** When there is evil in the earth or around us, there is an anger we feel
towards that evil. The same heart feels compassion and sympathy towards the victims.

**Love.** By our emotions, we feel love, deep care and affection.

**Jealousy.** Jealousy comes from love: a wish to protect what is ours, and a sense of wanting good
for ourselves. Yet it is easily corrupted when it puts others below itself.

**Patience and a good anger.** Patience helps us restrain ourselves from acting rashly. And
sometimes a good anger gives us the drive and audacity to attempt things our reasoning minds would
stop us from doing. In such situations, something we know can drive that anger.

**Regret and shame.** Regret makes us identify where we went wrong. Shame makes us question our
actions. It is a way to audit our actions and intentions, scrutinising what we ought to do and what
we ought not to.

**Hatred of evil, not of people.** How about hatred? When we see evil being done, we sense an urge of
energy and drive against it that pushes us to act towards correcting that evil. That is the purpose
of that emotion. Many people have pushed for certain evils in their countries to be overturned. Our
emotions played a significant part in seeking what is right. "Abhor that which is evil; cleave to
that which is good" (Romans 12:9).

## When emotions go wrong

We cannot pretend not to see the great evil done in this world as a result of wrong emotions.
Sociopaths, self-centred people, narcissists and many others have done more evil on this earth than
we can begin to imagine: wars, attacks, lives lost, and corrupt individuals destroying lives,
families and governments.

## The necessity of a good God

Because having our emotions isn't evil, there has to be a standard for us to strike the balance
between good and evil. Love is so pure and so genuine, yet it can drive people to hate and hurt
those who hurt what they love. This is why there is the necessity of a good God.

## The reality of God

This brings me again to the standard of evil. If there is no standard of morality, how then did our
laws come about? How do we tell right from wrong? How do we hold ourselves accountable for what
others do, and for what we ourselves do?

How did we inherently come to have a moral code to know right from wrong? We are able to tell
because of the absolute existence of a God who gives us moral instincts. God made us in His image and
likeness ("Let us make man in our image, after our likeness", Genesis 1:26), and wrote the work of
His law on our hearts (Romans 2:15), so that we naturally understand good and evil.

## Knowing the Giver

Our emotions are given for us to feel, and they are part of what makes us human. So how do we
understand what to do and what not to do? By knowing the Giver of the emotions: by attaining the
knowledge of a person, the Giver of our emotions, and His will concerning them.

## A closing question

So without our emotions, what is the difference between humans and machines, or other creatures?
